Sailing around the world: Bilibili vs YouTube

用一只帆船也可以环游世界？ · 小鹿Lawrence

Snapshot: September 17, 2026, Vancouver · YouTube ↗ · Bilibili ↗ · Previous comparison

Main finding: shared longing, different discussion formats. Both samples contain admiration and envy of an adventurous life. Bilibili’s visible replies develop that into a conversation about hardship, inequality, and coping. YouTube also contains appreciation of the filming and practical sailing questions.

This is not an English-versus-Chinese audience comparison. 90 of 92 retrieved YouTube comments are primarily Chinese. The upload has English subtitles, but only one retrieved comment is English. Platform differences here cannot establish national or language-group differences.

9. What this case actually tells us

  1. Shared emotion is stronger than a national-cultural split.

    The two discussions both turn adventure into life comparison. This pair does not provide enough English comments to test English-versus-Chinese reactions.

  2. Thread structure changes what becomes visible.

    A reply under an envy comment is invited to discuss envy. A reply under the creator’s like target is invited to participate. YouTube’s top-level reactions are a different unit.

  3. One creator can carry part of a community across platforms.

    Shared references and explicit cross-platform support challenge the idea of two independent audiences.

  4. To test culture, change the sampling design.

    Find a comparable upload with a substantial English-language comment sample, then compare language groups, equivalent thread levels, and matched time windows across more than one video.
