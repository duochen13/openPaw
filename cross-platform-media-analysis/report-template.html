Jeremy Grantham · The Diary Of A CEO · Comment snapshot: September 16, 2026, Vancouver

Main insight: Bilibili’s accessible threads focus on investment choices and credibility. YouTube’s featured comments also emphasize the speaker and everyday life. Both contain distrust of elite advice. Checking YouTube’s newest comments weakens a simple cultural contrast.

5. What the emotions may signal

  1. Admiration → perceived competence, not necessarily agreement.

    Praising the guest’s clarity does not prove acceptance of his market forecast.

  2. Anger about housing → exclusion and fairness.

    A sampled commenter welcomes a crash because they feel priced out. Negative emotion toward today’s economy can coexist with a positive view of falling prices.

  3. Skepticism → demand for consistency.

    The holdings objection on both platforms tests actions against words. Its cross-platform presence is stronger evidence than a claim that skepticism belongs to one culture.

  4. Irony and references → shared local context.

    The Bilibili financial-personality analogy conveys meaning through a familiar domestic reference. That is culturally situated; it does not establish a national personality trait.
