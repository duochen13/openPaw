# Gordon's dumplings get refused: Bilibili vs YouTube

Same program (The F Word), same premise: Gordon Ramsay cooks Chinese-style
dumplings and Chinese head chefs judge them. YouTube clip (4:12, "Gordon's
Dumplings Get Refused By Head Chef", 9.7M views, Jun 2022) vs Bilibili
re-upload (10:38, "戈登被中国点心师傅训自闭", 4.0M views).
Exact edit equivalence unverified.

## Samples

- YouTube: 100 top-level comments, Top sort, all English. Collected Oct 4,
2026 via browser transcription (YouTube bot-checks datacenter IPs).
- Bilibili: 409 records = 3 top-level roots + 406 replies, all Chinese.
Same hot-endpoint access limit as the sailing case (3 roots exposed).

## YouTube topic coding (100 comments, one primary topic each)

The top comment (104K likes): Gordon is the big dog in his own kitchen but
becomes the student again in someone else's — respectful and willing to learn.

## Bilibili threads

1. "这四个人的表情" (17,778 likes, 90 replies): amusement at the chefs'
faces → develops into professional critique (duck skin removed, ginger/
garlic overpowering the filling).
2. "案板厨师15年" (8,136 likes, 277 replies): a self-described 15-year
cutting-board chef anchors a technique deep-dive — shrimp-dumpling
filling, skin work, recipes, restaurant recommendations.
3. "戈登人还不错" (7,929 likes, 39 replies): character defense — Gordon
respects serious chefs; the harsh Hell's Kitchen persona is show effect.

## Findings

1. **Shared core, different doors in.** Both admire Gordon's humility and the
chefs' strict standards. YouTube states it as character praise (top
comment); Bilibili performs it through faces, craft talk, persona debate.
2. **The 45/100 thread is YouTube-specific** (11%). Bilibili barely mentions
the score — the "foreign" mediation is invisible under a Chinese title.
3. **Professional depth lives in Bilibili replies** (277-reply technique
exchange). YouTube's professional comments are shorter and fewer (7%).
4. **Clean language split** (YouTube 100% English, Bilibili 100% Chinese),
unlike the sailing case — but language still doesn't establish who
viewers are, and reply-vs-top-level structure remains a confound.
