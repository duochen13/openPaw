Beta, R² & alpha over time

Trailing 250-session OLS vs . A falling beta means the stock moves less with the market; alpha is the residual drift. The slope tracks whether alpha is improving while still negative. All describe the past; none is a forecast. Daily/weekly resolution covers the most recent 400 sessions; earlier history stays monthly.

β (beta)

β above 1 → more volatile than the index; below 1 → calmer. Dashed line at β = 1.0 marks market-neutral.

R²

Share of return variance explained by the benchmark — high = moves with the market, low = mostly idiosyncratic. Bounded 0–1. Dashed lines at 0.70 (β reasonably trustworthy) and 0.85 (strong index linkage).

α, annualized

α slope, per session

Change in annualized α per trading session, over 20 sessions (two-point: latest minus 20-sessions-ago, divided by 20). Slope > 0 → alpha is improving. Line shows a 5-point trailing average for readability; turnaround signals use the raw slope.

Turnaround signals

α < 0 and slope > 0 — turnaround watch: alpha is negative but improving.

P/E ratio over time

Daily close over trailing-twelve-month EPS — the sum of the four latest reported quarters. Gaps mark days before four quarters were reported, or with non-positive earnings: a P/E over losses is meaningless, not large. Valuation history, not a forecast.

Business metrics

Quarterly operating metrics from SEC EDGAR filings, plus hand-entered company-disclosed metrics — the numbers behind the price. Bars show the quarterly value; the lower line shows year-over-year change (percentage points for margins). Gaps mean the filer did not XBRL-tag that quarter. Reported history, not a forecast.

Triangles flag unusually large moves after adjusting for the benchmark; ◆ marks a dated, collected event (earnings, SEC filing, macro release) even when that day's move was modest. Events in the surrounding window are context, not proof of causation. This page shows collected evidence; model attribution and its reliability controls have not been run.
